# Clock coupling fixed by edge-rate control, not by spacing

A data line showed jitter and occasional bit errors where a clock ran nearby. Coupling was present, but the dominant fault was the clock's fast edge rate rather than a long parallel run: the two shared only a short adjacency, well under the length that drives far-end crosstalk. The fix added a series termination resistor on the clock to slow the edge and made a small timing-skew adjustment, which cleared the errors. Opening the spacing over the short overlap changed little. This is an edge-rate and timing fix, not the spacing-driven crosstalk seen on long high-speed parallel runs.
