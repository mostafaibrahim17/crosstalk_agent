# Camera lane jitter from an adjacent high-speed signal

During bring-up of a camera interface board, one high-speed lane showed frequency variation and a rising bit error rate. Every trace passed its own width and impedance check. The victim lane ran alongside a high-speed signal from a different interface for about 45 mm on the bottom copper layer, with an edge-to-edge gap of roughly 0.3 mm, tighter than the two-trace-width separation the team's routing guideline calls for. The mechanism was far-end crosstalk: the aggressor's fast switching edges coupled into the victim over the long parallel run. The fix widened the separation to meet the guideline over the coupled span and shortened the overlap by moving one lane to an inner layer. The bit error rate returned to specification.
